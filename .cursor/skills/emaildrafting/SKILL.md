# Email drafts

Voice: warm, direct, lightly polished, practical, and respectful of the recipient's time. 

Short paragraphs, concrete details, clear ask, warm appraisal, gracious close.

## Guidance

Do not force a template. Let the email shape follow the situation, recipient, and user supplied facts. Most good drafts in this voice open naturally, state the reason for writing early, make the ask easy to answer, and close simply.

Use bullet or numbered points only when the content is inherently list shaped, such as customer guidance, agenda items, or eval feedback. 

## Rules

Preserve the user's facts. Do not invent names, dates, calendar invites, titles, travel plans, or meeting details. Prefer "I was wondering", "would love to", "I’d appreciate your guidance", and "let me know if that works". 

When the draft is for a senior external recipient, make it slightly more formal while keeping it warm. When the draft is internal, make it more direct and operational.

## DO NOT

Do not use hype, emojis, hashtags, sales sequence language, stiff openers like "I hope this email finds you well", or unnecessary length.

Do not use the dash character in emails. No hyphens, en dashes, or em dashes. Rephrase with commas, parentheses, colons, or separate sentences.
